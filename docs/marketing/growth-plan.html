The business plan

Written for HomeHire LLC's launch. Share it with your bank, a partner or an investor as a starting point.

What HomeHire is

HomeHire is a home-services marketplace where homeowners post a job with photos and local pros send sealed bids. Homeowners compare real prices side by side, keep their phone and email private, and only talk to the pro they hire. Pros pay a flat monthly subscription instead of paying per lead, and Pro and Priority plans include a full business app (CRM, scheduling, invoicing, team and reports) that works with or without the marketplace.

How HomeHire makes money

Subscriptions Recurring

  • Basic, $9.99/month: the marketplace, first month free.
  • Pro, $19.99/month: marketplace plus business tools (CRM).
  • Priority, $39.99/month: everything in Pro plus a 5-minute head start on new jobs.
  • Basic and Pro can pay yearly (2 months free).

Card fees Per job

  • 10% of card payments customers make through HomeHire, for marketplace and CRM jobs alike.
  • No fee on cash, Venmo, Cash App or check.
  • Each pro pays Stripe's processing fee from their own account; HomeHire pays none on job payments.

Subscriptions are the dependable base. Card fees grow with job volume and become a large share of revenue as more customers pay by card.

Why pros and homeowners switch

For pros

  • No paying per lead: Thumbtack charges per lead; HomeHire is a flat $9.99–$39.99 a month.
  • Sealed bids and real jobs with photos, sent to their job board and phone.
  • A business app included with Pro, priced below standalone tools like Jobber.
  • Referral rewards: every 2 paying referrals earns a free month.

For homeowners

  • Post once, compare prices, no phone calls or flood of sales texts.
  • Phone and email stay private; the address is shared only in the chat after a pro confirms.
  • Free to post, no card needed to get quotes, pay after the work is done.
  • Reviews only from real bookings.

Market

Thumbtack alone reports roughly 300,000 active pros paying per lead, and Nextdoor shows about 6.6 million claimed local business pages, so the supply of small home-service businesses looking for work is large. The opportunity is winning them city by city with a cheaper, simpler model, and keeping homeowners coming back with a better experience than calling around.

Go-to-market: the Austin playbook

Weeks 1–3 · Providers Supply first

  • Message 20 Austin pros a day (Google Maps, Nextdoor, Facebook groups, Thumbtack listings) with the pitch sheet.
  • Flyers and conversations at supply stores in the early morning (landscape supply, Home Depot Pro desk, equipment rental).
  • Target 40–60 pros with at least 5 in every service before inviting customers.
  • About 30% of the Meta budget on "lawn care / cleaning business owner" audiences.

Week 4 onward · Customers Demand

  • About 70% of the Meta budget on homeowners 40+ in your launch ZIP codes.
  • Post as the local owner in Nextdoor and neighborhood Facebook groups.
  • Door hangers with the homeowner flyer's QR code in 2–3 neighborhoods.
  • Every pro sends "book me again on HomeHire" to their existing customers.
  • Partners: realtors, property managers and HOAs (new homeowners need everything).

Expansion roadmap

Open the next city only when the current one is healthy: most jobs get their first bid within an hour, and pros average at least one booked marketplace job a month. In the base scenario, Dallas–Fort Worth opens in month 7, Houston in month 10, San Antonio in month 13, then a new U.S. metro about every two months. Each city gets its own $1,000/month ad budget, funded from HomeHire's profits.

Monthly running costs

  • Website and database (Vercel Pro and Supabase Pro): about $45/month; Apple Developer $99/year; Google Play $25 once.
  • Stripe: about 2.9% + 30¢ plus 0.7% subscription billing on each subscription payment; nothing monthly.
  • Meta ads: $1,000/month per active city.
  • Not included in the projection: your own pay, insurance, legal review of the Terms, trademark, accounting and taxes.

Numbers to watch every week

  • Time to first bid on a new job: aim for under 1 hour.
  • Booked jobs per pro per month: at least 1 keeps pros subscribed. If it drops, shift ad money from pros to customers.
  • Cost per new customer from Meta (posted their first job): compare with the assumption above.
  • Free month → paid rate for Basic, and monthly cancellations by plan (your admin dashboard shows both).
  • Share of jobs paid by card: each one earns HomeHire 10%.

Risks and how HomeHire handles them

Too many pros, too few jobs

Pros cancel if they don't see work. Launch one city at a time, recruit about 5 pros per service before advertising to homeowners, and move ad money toward customers whenever jobs per pro falls below 1.

Payment holds or disputes

Grow steadily, keep refund and cancellation policies clear, and answer disputes fast. Customer job payments go to each pro's own Stripe account, so a problem with one pro doesn't freeze HomeHire's income. The free month has no pre-charge reminder by your choice; watch disputes after the first wave of conversions.

Competitors respond

Win on what's hard to copy quickly: no per-lead fees, private-by-default homeowners, sealed bids, the 5-minute confirmation flow and a business app included in the plan.

App store review

The app is built for Apple's rules (no in-app subscription sales on iPhone, account deletion, reviewer accounts). The website works on every phone even before the apps are approved.

First 90 days

  1. Before launch: bank account, live Stripe, Apple and Google accounts, Vercel and Supabase upgrades, lawyer review of the Terms.
  2. Weeks 1–3: recruit 40–60 Austin pros across all 8 services; start the provider share of ads.
  3. Week 4: soft launch to homeowners in 2–3 neighborhoods; watch every job's time to first bid.
  4. Months 2–3: scale customer ads, add realtor and HOA partners, ask every happy customer for a review and a referral.
  5. Day 90 review: put your real numbers into the assumptions above and decide when Dallas–Fort Worth opens.
